The decision panel is the only raised card on the page. Everything else — evidence, context, the advisory, the audit trail — is flat. The advisory in particular is dashed and sits on the page colour, so a reviewer skimming the case can never mistake the machine's opinion for the decision that was recorded.
“What this will do” is written before the decision, not after. Naming the downstream effect on attendance, reward eligibility and analytics inclusion is what makes a reason field meaningful — the reviewer is agreeing to a consequence, not filling in a box.
Every view is logged, and the reviewer is told so. The evidence footer names the log entry and the viewer-session expiry. Surveillance that is disclosed is accountability; surveillance that is silent is a surprise in an audit.
